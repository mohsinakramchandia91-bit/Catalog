What the comparison shows

  • Upload cadence. Videos per week or month, and how steady it is. Sporadic brilliance usually loses to reliable rhythm — the comparison shows you the rhythm that wins in your niche.
  • Typical performance. Average and median views per video — the honest “what does a normal upload earn them?”, stripped of one viral outlier.
  • Breakout videos. The uploads that did several times the channel's average, lined up so you can study what they share: topic angle, title construction, length, timing.
  • Title patterns. How the channel phrases titles — questions vs. statements, numbers, brackets, keyword placement — the packaging habits behind the clicks.
  • Catalogue shape. How much of their library is search-driven evergreen versus trend-chasing — and where the unserved gaps in the niche are hiding.

How to use it without fooling yourself

  1. Pick the right rival. Compare yourself with the channel one step ahead — close enough that their playbook fits your resources — and glance at the niche leader only for direction.
  2. Compare like with like. A channel with ten times your subscribers should average more views. Judge the patterns (cadence, per-video packaging, breakout rate), not just the raw totals.
  3. Extract decisions, not envy. Turn each difference into an experiment: “Their winners all answer one specific question — I'll test that format twice this month.”
  4. Feed it forward. Take the topics their audience proved it wants into Keyword Lab, check the scores, and make your own version — better aimed, in your voice. Then audit your result with Video X-Ray.

Good to know (honest limits)

  • Competitor Compare uses public data only. A rival's revenue, retention curves, CTR and traffic sources are visible only to that channel's owner — any tool claiming to show them is guessing or worse.
  • Averages hide stories: a channel propped up by one viral video looks stronger than it is. That's why the comparison includes medians and breakout analysis, not just averages.
  • Patterns are evidence, not instructions. What worked for their audience, budget and back catalogue may not transfer wholesale — test, don't transplant.
  • Everything runs through the official API on your own free access: API key (your own Google project) or Google sign-in (OnyxIQ project — Google verification in progress).

Frequently asked questions

What can competitor analysis actually tell me?

The public mechanics of a channel's success: how often they upload, what an average video earns them, which videos broke out and what those videos have in common — topics, title constructions, lengths — and where the gaps are that nobody in the niche is serving. It turns “they're just better” into a list of observable choices.

Which channels should I compare?

Two kinds: channels one step ahead of you in your niche (close enough that their playbook is realistic for you) and the niche leader (to see where the ceiling is). Comparing yourself only with the biggest channel in the niche mostly produces despair, not decisions.

Is this just copying competitors?

No — and it shouldn't become that. Analysis shows you the patterns a niche rewards: the questions viewers want answered, the title shapes that earn clicks, the cadence that sustains growth. You still make your own videos with your own voice. Borrow the map, not the territory.

What data does Competitor Compare use?

Public channel and video data from the official YouTube Data API v3 — uploads, view counts, titles, publish dates. Private numbers (a rival's revenue, retention, traffic sources) are not public and no legitimate tool can show them to you.
